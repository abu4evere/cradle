# C1-B: a sibling. Does a younger child learn faster with an older sibling to watch? (DRAFT, not frozen)

**Why:**
- The creator chose "a mix" for the other children: AI adults, plus a few neural children as peers.
- C1-B is the first rung with a second neural child, an older sibling.
- Real toddlers learn a great deal by watching older siblings. C1 showed the child can keep up with routines (with
  memory). C1-B asks whether another child's turns teach it, and how.
- It is also the scorecard's Part A #4, "learning from others", in a life rather than a toy.

## The home
- **C1's home:** the same 30 things, 6 routines over 12 shared things, the same noise, the same parents and corpus.
  Days are C1's (seeded).
- **Two children: an older sibling and a younger child.**
  - **The older** first lives 360 days alone, exactly as C1's W child. Then the younger is born and both live 720 more
    days. The older keeps learning by C1's rules.
  - **The younger** is C1's W child (a window of the last 4 things used), from day 0.
- **Turns:** each step that was "the child's turn" in C1 goes to one of the two children, by a fair coin from the day's
  stream (the same draws in every arm).
- **Everyone hears what the parents say,** as in C1.

## The arms (they differ only in what the younger sees of its sibling)
| arm | the younger's life |
|---|---|
| **A** | no sibling: every child's turn is its own (C1's W child, the baseline for a single child) |
| **Y−** | a sibling exists, but its turns happen out of the younger's sight: the younger neither sees them nor hears the parent's answer. The younger gets about half the turns. This is the control for having fewer turns |
| **Y** | the younger sees the sibling's turns: the thing handed is a used thing (in its window and its prediction learning) and it hears the answer. Its act learns only from its own turns, as in C1 |
| **Yi** | as Y, plus **imitation**: when the sibling is praised, the younger also nudges its own act toward the thing the sibling handed, as if it had been its own right turn (the same learning rate) |

24 younger children per arm (seeds 1–24), paired across arms. The older sibling of seed s is the same child at its
day 360 in every arm.

## Tests (the younger, alone, learning frozen; C1's 30 test days)
- **When:** days 7, 14, 30, 60, 90, 180, 360, 720 of the younger's life.
- **What:** C1's two measures, clean and noisy: turn-taking (hands the right thing on its turn) and anticipation.

## Readings (to be frozen)
- **Primary, early learning speed:** the mean clean turn accuracy over the tests of days 7–90.
  - **"Watching helps"** if Y − Y− has a lower bound above 0.
  - **"Imitation helps"** if Yi − Y has a lower bound above 0.
- **Final level:** day 720, Y and Yi against A (does sharing turns with a sibling cost anything in the end?).
- **The older:** its turn accuracy before and after the younger arrives (does a younger sibling slow it?). This is
  descriptive.
- **Statistics:** paired by seed; 95% bootstrap intervals over the 24 seeds (10,000 draws, NumPy seed 20261117).

## Open questions for the council
1. Is the imitation rule (Yi) a fair, simple form of learning from others?
2. Is Y− a fair control for fewer turns?
3. Should the older sibling stop learning once the younger is born (a fixed model), or keep learning (as drafted)?

## The assistant's prior
- Watching helps (Y − Y− above 0): about 60%.
- Imitation helps (Yi − Y above 0): about 55%.
- Y at day 720 below A: about 30%.
