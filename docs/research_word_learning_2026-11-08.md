# Research notes: how children (and networks) learn first words (8 November 2026)

For C0 (`C0_plan_draft.md`). A source suggests; only a frozen test decides. **What was read:** the search-result record
and abstract text for each source below (web search, 8 November 2026). No full text was read.

1. **Bergelson, E. and Swingley, D. (2012).** "At 6–9 months, human infants know the meanings of many common nouns."
   *PNAS* 109(9): 3253–3258. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1113380109
   - Record: 33 infants aged 6–9 months showed they knew words for foods and body parts, learned from daily
     experience, and generalised across instances (a noun names a category, not one object).
   - For C0: a descriptive anchor for when real children understand their first nouns.
2. **Smith, L. and Yu, C. (2008).** "Infants rapidly learn word-referent mappings via cross-situational statistics."
   *Cognition* 106(3): 1558–1568. https://philpapers.org/rec/SMIIRL
   - Record: infants track which word goes with which object across many ambiguous moments, not only from one clear
     moment.
   - For C0: the reason arm D (no joint attention) is not hopeless. Learning across moments is a real human route.
3. **Vong, W. K., Wang, W., Orhan, A. E. and Lake, B. M. (2024).** "Grounded language acquisition through the eyes and
   ears of a single child." *Science* 383: 504–511. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1374
   - Record: a network (CVCL) trained on one child's head-camera video and the speech heard (ages 6–25 months)
     learned word–referent pairs and generalised to new examples. It combined learning to see with learning
     associations.
   - For C0: the closest published model to C0's child brain (a dual encoder for words and sights). C0 differs because
     its child acts and is rewarded inside a world, and is not only trained on a recording.
