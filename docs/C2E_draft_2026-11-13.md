# C2-E: experts and costly practice. When does choosing whom to copy pay? (DRAFT for the council, 13 November 2026)

**The creator chose this rung** (13 November 2026, "yes go ahead with the next cradle rung"), after C2-S.

**Why:** C2-S found that the child learns whom to trust, but that being selective does not beat "copy everyone and
practise", because its own practice quickly corrects bad copies. Two things C2-S left out are common in real life:
1. **Practice is costly:** you cannot try everything many times.
2. **People are experts in some things and not others:** a good cook is not a good mechanic.

C2-E asks whether selective trust pays when these are present, and whether trust must be **per person and per kind of
task** (who is good at *what*).

## The task (C2-S's boxes)
- 6 kinds of box; each opens by its own ordered pair of acts.
- Demonstrations on days 1–30, none on days 31–60.

## What is new
1. **Experts:** Ann is good at kinds 0–2 and Ben at kinds 3–5.
   - On their own kinds they show the right pair 85% of the time.
   - On the others, 25% of the time.
   - Otherwise each shows their own fixed wrong habit for that kind (drawn per seed).
   - Overall, both are equally reliable; only per-kind trust can tell them apart.
2. **Practice:** each child is in one of two conditions.
   - **Full:** 6 own tries a day, one per kind (as C2-S).
   - **Scarce:** 1 own try a day (the kind cycles through all 6). Demonstrations still come before every one of the 6
     daily slots (watching is cheap; doing is costly). The copy step happens at every demonstration.
3. **Results seen** in half of the demonstrations (as C2-S).

## The child
C2-S's network, with who (2), the demonstrated acts, result seen, opened, own first act and step. REINFORCE on its own
tries. The copy step is at half rate × the weight w.

## Arms (48 children each; each arm under full and under scarce practice)
| arm | copy weight |
|---|---|
| T | no demonstrations |
| I | watches, no copy |
| Cb | blind (w = 1) |
| Ct | trust per person, from seen results (C2-S) |
| Ctk | trust per person **and kind**, from seen results |
| Cyk | yoked control: Ctk's current weight for a coin-chosen person (and the same kind) |
| Cp | trust per person, from own results after copying (C2-S) |
| Cpk | trust per person and kind, from own results after copying |

Trust starts at 0.5, rate 0.3, w = trust / max(trust of the two people for that kind (or overall), 0.25).

## Readings (to be frozen; early learning = mean success at days 1, 2, 3, 5, 7; also day 30)
1. **"Per-kind trust beats copying everyone when practice is scarce":** Ctk − Cb (scarce) has a lower bound above 0.
2. **"…and it is the person-and-kind choice, not the amount of copying":** Ctk − Cyk (scarce) has a lower bound
   above 0.
3. **"Scarcity raises the value of choosing":** (Ctk − Cb)scarce − (Ctk − Cb)full has a lower bound above 0.
4. **"Who is good at what, not just who":** Ctk − Ct (scarce) has a lower bound above 0.
5. **"From own experience":** Cpk − Cb (scarce) has a lower bound above 0.

## Open questions for the council
1. Is "scarce practice" (1 try a day against 6) the right way to make practice costly? Or should a failed try cost
   something (a penalty) instead?
2. Are 85% and 25% fair numbers for "expert" and "non-expert"?
3. With scarce practice, own-experience trust (Cp, Cpk) updates rarely. Is it fair to include it, or does it fail by
   design?
4. Is the early window still right, or should day 30 be primary when practice is scarce?
