## Round 1
**The main flaw, found by all three: Ct − Co confounds trust with the amount of copying.**
- Co skips every hidden demonstration, while Ct copies them all (weighted).
- So Ct may win "simply because it copies more".

**The fixes they proposed:**
- GPT: a yoked control, Cy, with the same copy weight and timing, assigned independently of who demonstrated;
- Gemini: a rate-matched Co (Co_2x);
- DeepSeek: a fixed-weight copier of hidden demonstrations.

**Also from all three:** trust learned from the child's **own** results after copying someone is the stronger test.
- GPT: "Cp succeeding would be the more interesting result".
- Gemini: "it requires no result-seen flag, works under 100% hidden outcomes".
- DeepSeek: "more defensible".

**Points from one model:**
- **GPT:**
  - visibility strata (0%, 50% and 100% hidden);
  - measure selective copying directly (Ann against Ben);
  - Ca − Cb shows the cost of a bad model, not learned selection;
  - a feedforward network cannot infer reliability over time, so Ct's trust variable supplies that memory.
- **Gemini:**
  - **trust at rate 0.1 is too slow** for the early window. The assistant checked: after about 10 seen results, Ann
    reaches about 0.83 and Ben 0.29 by day 7. It proposed 0.25–0.30;
  - zero the demonstration inputs during the child's own try. **Corrected in round 2:** that would make I equal to T
    by construction; in C2 the demonstration stays in view during the try.
- **DeepSeek:**
  - Ben, with a fixed wrong habit used 80% of the time, is "consistently bad" rather than "unreliable";
  - his 20% right demonstrations may teach the method;
  - copy fidelity per demonstrator;
  - power (24 seeds).

**Probabilities:**
- GPT: Ca − Cb 75%, Co − Cb 70%, Ct − Co 50%;
- DeepSeek: reading 3 about 35–40% until the confound is fixed;
- Gemini: none.

## Round 2 (the assistant's resolution put to all three)
**The resolution:**
- the yoked control Cy;
- the own-experience arm Cp, plus Cb100 and Cp100 with every result hidden;
- copy weight = trust relative to the most trusted person;
- trust rate 0.3;
- 48 seeds;
- **Ben keeps his fixed habit at 80%**, his right demonstrations are reported, and imperfect or varying models go to
  the next rung.

**All three accept the resolution on Ben.**
- DeepSeek: call him "consistently wrong", so reading 1 is about the harm of a bad model, not about unreliability. Taken.

**Further corrections, all taken:**
- **Cy:**
  - GPT: shuffling within a day moves weights across box kinds.
  - **The fix:** at each demonstration, Cy uses Ct's *current* weight for a person chosen by a fair coin (fixed draws),
    so the slot, the time and the kind are kept and only the link to who demonstrated is cut.
- **Cp's credit assignment:**
  - Gemini: the child's own skill after copying Ben would be credited to Ben.
  - GPT: success right after copying is not necessarily caused by the copy; add a control with identities shuffled in
    the trust update.
  - **The fixes:**
    - Cp updates trust in a person **only when its try used the pair that person showed** ("copying them paid off or
      not");
    - **a control, Cpx,** credits the same update to a person chosen by a fair coin.
- **Normalisation:** Gemini, a first-mover penalty and 0/0. The copy weight becomes trust / max(max trust, 0.25),
  and the early trust trajectories are logged.
  - GPT: normalisation guarantees full copying of someone even when both are bad. Acceptable here (a test of relative
    selection, not of whether copying pays).
- **Power:** GPT. 48 seeds improves precision without guaranteeing power; effect sizes are reported.
- **A negative reading 1** (if Ben's right demonstrations teach the method): DeepSeek. Read as "not shown", not as
  failed.
