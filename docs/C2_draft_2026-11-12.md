# C2: learning how. Can the child learn a method by watching, when the result alone does not show it? (DRAFT)

**Why:**
- C1-B found that a younger child learns routines from its sibling just by seeing what happens. Copying and overheard
  praise added nothing, because the outcome (which thing was used) already showed everything.
- Much of what children learn from others is not "what" but **"how"**: a sequence of acts whose result does not
  reveal it.
- The classic contrast (Horner & Whiten 2005) is **imitation** (copying the acts) against **emulation** (reproducing
  the result by one's own means).
- This is also the scorecard's Part A #4, "learning from others", in a task where it can show.

## The home (C1's, plus toy boxes)
- **Toy boxes:** 6 kinds of box among the home's things. Each opens only by a fixed **sequence of 2 acts** on it, from
  4 acts (press, pull, turn, shake): 16 possible sequences. Each kind has its own sequence (drawn once per seed).
  Inside is a treat (reward 1).
- **Episodes:** a few times a day, a box is in view and the child may try. A try is 2 acts, then the box opens or not.
- **Demonstrations:** before some tries, a demonstrator opens a box of the same kind. The arms differ in what the child
  sees of it.

## The child
- C1's W child, plus an **act head**: 4 act scores, from the box's look and the child's memory window, where the last
  acts it has seen or done are kept.
- Learning: REINFORCE on its own tries (the treat as reward), as C1's act learning.

## The arms (24 children each; paired)
| arm | what the child sees of a demonstration |
|---|---|
| **T** | no demonstrations (trial and error only) |
| **E** (emulation) | the box opening, but not the acts |
| **I** (imitation) | the two acts in order, and the box opening; the acts enter its memory window |
| **Ic** (imitation + copying) | as I, plus a copy step: its act head is nudged toward the seen acts (half rate, as C1-B's Yi) |
| **Iw** (wrong demonstrator) | as Ic, but a third of the demonstrations use a wrong sequence that fails (does it copy blindly?) |

## Measures
- Tries to the first success per box kind.
- Success rate per box kind by day.
- In Iw, whether the child copies failed demonstrations (selective copying, as GPT asked for the scorecard).

## Readings (to be frozen)
- **"Watching the method helps"** if I − E on the success rate in the first 30 days has a lower bound above 0.
- **"Copying helps"** if Ic − I has a lower bound above 0.
- **"Selective"** if, in Iw, copying after failed demonstrations is lower than after successful ones.

## Open questions for the council
1. Is a 2-act sequence out of 16 the right difficulty (trial and error alone takes about 16 tries per box)?
2. Is the demonstrator a parent (an AI adult) or the older sibling (C1-B's)?
3. Should the child be able to see the box's inside (the result) at all in E?

## The assistant's prior
- Watching the method helps (I − E): about 65%.
- Copying helps (Ic − I): about 60%.
- Selective copying in Iw: about 40%.
