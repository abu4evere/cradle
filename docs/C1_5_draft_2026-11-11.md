# C1.5: word order. Can the child tell "X then Y" from "Y then X", and "not X" from "X"? (DRAFT, not frozen)

Written 11 November 2026, in sleep mode, as the next rung named in C1's findings (council 4). For the council, then a
freeze.

## Why this rung
- **The child hears a bag of words.** C0 to C1 heard a sentence as the mean of its word embeddings. So "give the cup,
  then the spoon" and "give the spoon, then the cup" are the same input, and so are "the cup" and "not the cup … the
  spoon" when the same words occur.
- **C0-B found this already:** correction hurt, and the bag-of-words hearing is the likely cause.
- **"No" and "first … then"** are among the first structured words in a real toddler's life, so the life ladder needs
  them before C2 (other people).

## The home
As C1: the same 30 things and families, the same two AI parents, the same routines. One thing is new: **requests.**
- **Requests:** at a child's turn, with chance ½, the parent asks instead of waiting. Three kinds, equally likely:
  - **two in order:** "the X, then the Y" (and paraphrases). The child must hand X, then Y;
  - **negation:** "not the X, the Y" / "no, the Y". The child must hand Y, and handing X is wrong;
  - **plain:** "the X". As C1.
- **The key pairs:** every order request has a twin with X and Y swapped, so a bag-of-words child cannot score above
  ½ on order. That is built into the test, not left to chance.
- **The frames** come from a committed corpus, as C0-A's (made once by the parents' language model, checked, then
  fixed). The child never contains a language model.

## The child (arms differ only in how a sentence is heard)
| arm | hearing |
|---|---|
| **B** | the mean of word embeddings (C1's; the control) |
| **P** | each word's embedding plus a learned position embedding, then the mean (order as position) |
| **Q** | a small recurrent reader (Elman, 16 units) over the words in order; its last state is the sentence |
| **Q−** | Q, with the words shuffled within each sentence before reading (the order control) |

The memory across steps is C1's window W in every arm, as C1 found it as good as R on these routines and simpler.

## Measures (at the end of life, 720 days; 16 children per arm)
- **Primary:** order accuracy on held-out twin pairs (both orders right), Q − B.
- **Negation:** the share of negated requests where the named-but-negated thing is *not* handed.
- **Secondary:** plain-request accuracy (must not drop against C1); learning speed (days 7, 30, 60, 180, 360, 720).
- **Readings (to be frozen):**
  - "hears order" if Q − B's lower bound is above 0 and Q − Q−'s lower bound is above 0;
  - "position is enough" if P does as well as Q;
  - "not learned" otherwise.
