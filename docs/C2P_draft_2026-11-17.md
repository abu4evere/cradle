# C2-P: whom to copy when mistakes hurt (DRAFT)

**Why:**
- **C2-K3** gave the first clear "yes" on choosing whom to copy, at a matched amount of copying: Ctk − Cyk (G)
  +0.069 [+0.035, +0.104].
- **But copying everyone (Cb) was as good as choosing** (Ctk − Cb +0.010). The findings' conclusion: selective trust
  should beat copying everyone when a wrong copy is costly, which was untested.
- **Mind Lab's W3h** showed that pain (an immediate cost on a harmful act) changes what a brain learns: it cut harm by
  40%, and it kept a parent's lesson from fading.
- **So C2-P puts a cost on wrong acts.** A child who copies everyone also copies Ben's wrong habits on Ann's kinds, and
  must then pay for trying them. Choosing should now pay.

## The design: C2-K3 unchanged (WS's memory brain, boxes, experts, showings, arms, level K2), plus two costs
| condition | what a wrong lid costs |
|---|---|
| **G** (C2-K3's) | the try ends (no cost beyond losing the rest of the box) |
| **GP** (pain) | the try ends, and the reward −p at that step (p = 0.5: half of what a whole box pays) |
| **GS** (scarce) | the try ends, and that kind's remaining tries that day are lost (a broken box) |

- **Arms:** T, I, Cb, Ctk, Cyk, Cok (C2-K3's six; Cr dropped).
- **Children:** 48 per arm and condition, seeds 1–48. G is re-run, so all three conditions share code and seeds.
- **Pain is logged:** every child's total pain, and its pain per try.

## Readings (day-30 full-box success; paired bootstrap; n = 48)
1. **PRIMARY:** (Ctk − Cb) in GP, lower bound above 0. When mistakes hurt, choosing beats copying everyone.
2. (Ctk − Cb) in GS, lower bound above 0. When chances are few, choosing beats copying everyone.
3. **The interaction:** (Ctk − Cb)GP − (Ctk − Cb)G, lower bound above 0.
4. **Pain paid to learn:** Ctk − Cb on total pain over days 1–30 in GP, upper bound below 0.
- **Reported:**
  - day 60;
  - Cok − Cb;
  - Ctk − Cyk in each condition;
  - whether pain also slows the trust-learning itself.

## Open questions for the council
1. Is p = 0.5 a fair cost? Too high may stop all trying (fear); too low changes nothing.
2. Is "a broken box loses the day's remaining tries of that kind" a fair model of scarce chances?
3. Should I (watching without copying) be kept? It was near 0 in C2-K3.
4. Any trap where Ctk wins only because it copies less (fewer copies, fewer wrong habits) rather than because it
   chooses well? Cyk (C2-K3's matched-copying control) is kept for that.
