## The question (sent as one paragraph)
Council (Cradle, a sister project of ACL, 10 November 2026). Cradle raises a newborn neural-network child (never a language model) among AI parents and asks, stage by stage like an ordinary human life, whether it keeps up. Done: C0-A and C0-B (word learning; a small network that hears a sentence as the mean of learned word embeddings and sees things as 16-number look vectors; association plus REINFORCE). C0-B (30 things in look-alike families, sparse hearings) found brain size and joint attention matter for learning speed, late growth does not help, and correction hurts because hearing is bag-of-words. That child has no state carried between moments. Draft C1, routines (ages 1-3): one home, the same AI parents and fixed corpus; each day is a sequence of 3-5 routines drawn from a fixed set (waking, breakfast, dressing, going out, bath, bedtime), each a fixed ordered chain of 3-5 steps; each step shows what is in view, what the parent does, and whose turn it is; nobody labels the routine (no sentence names a routine or uses order words); noise: some days a step is skipped, two steps swap, or a routine is cut short. At child-turn steps the child picks one act from a small set (hand over the thing the step needs, wait, reach, nothing). Measures: (1) taking its turn = share of child-turn steps with the right act; (2) anticipating = before the parent acts, the share of steps where the child's top predicted next thing is the one that comes (a read-out of its state, never taught). Arms (16 seeds each): N = C0-B's best brain, no state (sham); R = same size plus a recurrent state vector; P = same size plus the previous step's input as extra senses (memory without recurrence); S = arm R with steps shuffled within routines each day (control). Readings: 'keeps up' if R or P is above chance on both measures and above N on anticipation with a lower bound above 0, and S is not above chance; 'memory is not enough' if R beats N on anticipation but not on turns; else 'no improvement shown'. The creator decided the other children are a mix: AI adults plus a few neural children as peers. Please answer briefly and concretely: (a) is prediction a sound read-out of knowing a routine, or should only the act count? (b) should the child's act be rewarded by the parent's response (the routine continuing, praise) or only by the body's own state, given that reward-only learning was far too slow in C0-B? (c) which memory: recurrent state, a window of past steps, or both, and what learning rule for the recurrent arm (backprop through time over how many steps)? (d) should C1 already include a neural sibling (the creator's mix), or does turn-taking with a parent come first? (e) the hearing is bag-of-words; should C1 also give the child word order (so it can understand 'no' and 'first...then'), or keep that for a separate rung? (f) your probability (0-100%) that R or P 'keeps up' while N does not.
