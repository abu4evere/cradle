# C2-S: copy who succeeds. Can the child learn whom to copy? (DRAFT for the council, 13 November 2026)

**The creator chose this rung** (13 November 2026, from four options): "Copy who succeeds".

**Why:**
- C2 showed the child learns a method only by copying it, and that its copy is blind: it copies failed demonstrations
  too, and its own practice corrects it.
- Building on what others learned (Bob's cumulative mind) needs more than copying. It needs **copying the right
  people**.
- **In real life:**
  - 14-month-olds imitate a model who used familiar objects competently, and not one who used them wrongly (Zmyj et al.
    2010);
  - preschoolers learn from an accurate speaker over an inaccurate one (Koenig & Harris 2005);
  - in a large simulation tournament, copying strategies won because the people copied tend to show what works for them
    (Rendell et al. 2010).

## The task (C2's toy boxes, unchanged)
- 6 kinds of box; each opens by its own ordered pair of acts (4 acts, 16 pairs). 6 tries a day, one per kind.
- 60 days: demonstrations on days 1–30, none on days 31–60 (retention).

## What is new: two demonstrators
- **Before each try on days 1–30, one of two older children demonstrates** a box of the same kind. Who demonstrates is
  an equal-chance draw, the same draws in every arm.
  - **Ann (reliable):** always uses the right pair, and the box opens.
  - **Ben (unreliable):** has his own wrong habit for each kind (a fixed wrong pair, drawn per seed). He uses it 80% of
    the time (the box stays shut) and the right pair 20% (it opens).
- **Hidden results:** in half of the demonstrations (equal-chance draws, the same in every arm), the child sees who and
  the acts but **not whether the box opened**.
- **The child recognises people:** it sees who is demonstrating (2 numbers, one-hot). Recognising a face is taken as
  inborn.

## The child (C2's network and learning)
- **Input:** look 16, who 2, the demonstrated acts 4 + 4, result seen 1, opened 1, its own first act 4, step 1 (33).
- **Network:** 33 → 32 tanh → 4.
- **Learning:** REINFORCE on its own tries (C2's rate). The copy step, where an arm has one, is one cross-entropy step
  toward the demonstrated acts at half rate (C2's).

## Arms (24 children each, seeds 1–24, paired: the same boxes, habits, demonstrators, hidden draws and days)
| arm | copy step | role |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | none, and no demonstrations | floor |
| **I** | none (it watches everything) | watching only (C2: teaches little) |
| **Cb** | copies every demonstration (blind, as C2's Ic) | blind copying |
| **Co** | copies only demonstrations it **saw open** | an inborn filter: copy what was seen to work |
| **Ct** | copies every demonstration, **scaled by its trust in that person**. Trust is the running mean of the outcomes it has seen from that person: it starts at 0.5, updates at rate 0.1, and is not updated by hidden results | an inborn mechanism with learned content: whom to trust |
| **Ca** | only Ann ever demonstrates; blind copy | ceiling: copying with no bad model |

## Readings (to be frozen)
**Statistics:** early learning = the mean success at the tests of days 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 (C2's). Differences are paired by
seed, with bootstrap intervals over the 24 seeds.
1. **"A bad model harms blind copying":** Ca − Cb has a lower bound above 0.
2. **"Copying only what works helps":** Co − Cb has a lower bound above 0.
3. **"Trusting the reliable person helps when results are hidden":** Ct − Co has a lower bound above 0.

**Descriptive:**
- **Adopting Ben's habit:** the share of the child's tries on days 1–30 that use Ben's wrong pair for that kind, per
  arm, against T's spontaneous rate.
- **Retention** at day 60.
- **Ct's final trust** in Ann and in Ben.

**Not at ceiling:** if T's early learning is 0.90 or more, no reading is drawn.

## Open questions for the council
1. **Co's filter and Ct's trust are hand-built mechanisms,** though what Ct trusts is learned. Is that fair, as W2's
   engineered memory was? Or should the child learn whom to copy with no built-in rule?
   - One middle option: trust learned from the child's **own** results after copying someone ("copying Ann paid off"),
     which works even when results are always hidden.
2. Is Ben well designed: a consistent wrong habit, right 20% of the time?
3. Is "half the results hidden" the right way to separate trusting a person from filtering by outcome?
4. What would make a pass meaningless?

## The assistant's prior
- A bad model harms blind copying: about 70%. Own practice partly corrects it, as in C2.
- Copying only what works helps: about 60%.
- Trust helps when results are hidden: about 55%.
