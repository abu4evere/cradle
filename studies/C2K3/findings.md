## The frozen readings (day-30 full-box success)
1. **PRIMARY. "Choosing the right expert pays when skills build on each other": yes.** Ctk − Cyk (G) +0.069 [+0.035,
   +0.104]. With the same amount of copying, aiming trust at the right person for each kind beats aiming it at random.
2. **"The oracle beats copying everyone": no.** Cok − Cb (G) −0.017 [−0.059, +0.021].
3. **"Learned choosing beats copying everyone": no.** Ctk − Cb (G) +0.010 [−0.021, +0.042].
4. **"The chain raises the value of choosing": no.** +0.014 [−0.042, +0.069].

## Reported (no gate)
- **The selection effect grows with time, and holds without the chain too:**
  - Ctk − Cyk (G) at day 60 is +0.188 [+0.139, +0.240];
  - Ctk − Cyk (U) is +0.056 at day 30 and +0.125 at day 60;
  - Cok − Cr: +0.063 (G) and +0.090 (U) at day 30; +0.146 (G) at day 60.
- **Copying everyone still matches choosing:**
  - Ctk − Cb (G) at day 60 is +0.042 [+0.003, +0.080], just above zero;
  - Cok − Cb is about 0 everywhere.
- **Watching with memory but no copying teaches almost nothing:** I stays at 0.003 to 0.007 at day 30.

## What it means (the assistant's reading)
1. **The first clear "yes" on choosing whom to copy, at a matched amount of copying.** Pointing trust at who is good at
   what, rather than at random people, gives a child more skill, and the gap widens after teaching stops.
2. **But the same old wall stands:** copying everyone and practising is as good as choosing, by day 30. A wrong copy is
   still cheap to undo for this brain, even with 2 lids, and choosing means copying less.
3. **The chain itself does not change this** (reading 4). Selection helps in flat tasks as much as in cumulative
   ones.
4. **Across C2-S, C2-E, C2-K and C2-K3, a consistent picture:**
   - the child **learns whom to trust**;
   - **whom it trusts matters**, against random trust;
   - but **copying more** is about as good as **copying the right people**.

   Selective trust should beat volume when wrong copies are costly: dangerous mistakes, or limited chances to learn at
   all. That is an untested condition.
